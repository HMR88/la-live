Assumptions and method
  • Road colours are absolute speeds, not a ratio to a baseline (that is step 6 of the plan).
  • Where a Caltrans detector sits on a segment, its speed is shown (cars, deprecated ATMS feed). Elsewhere the colour is the speed of buses between stops: pairs of vehicle positions 30 s apart projected onto the route shape, with pairs near a stop, under 20 m, over 120 s apart or over 110 km/h dropped. Buses are slower than cars; a bus segment at 20 km/h is normal.
  • A segment's value is the length-weighted mean of the last 10 minutes. Hover a road to see the source (bus, vds, both) and both speeds.
  • Vehicles: positions as the agencies publish them, up to 5 minutes old, LA Metro over its WebSocket, others polled every 30 s. On the GitHub Pages copy the snapshots come from a workflow that runs about every 5 minutes, so the "source" row says so and the positions are as old as the snapshot.
  • Events: CHP incidents of the Los Angeles dispatch, Caltrans lane closures current now, in-service message signs. Camera stills come from Caltrans when a popup opens; nothing is proxied.
  • Camera counts: a separate sampler opens one Caltrans stream at a time, decodes 15 s, tracks vehicles with YOLO11 small and ByteTrack, and counts tracks crossing a fixed line in each direction. A round of the subset runs at most every 5 minutes. Directions A and B are the two sides of that line, not route directions. Flow is mean track length in pixels per second, relative only. Speed needs a per-camera ground calibration; none exists yet, so no speed is shown. Counts do not colour roads (step 6). Some streams loop old footage; the burned-in clock on the frame is the only sign, see docs/log/step7.md.
  • Terrain is the AWS Terrain Tiles DEM with 1.3 times exaggeration. Buildings are OSM footprints extruded by height, else levels times 3.5 m, else 8 m.
