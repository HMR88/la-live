      Speed on roads

      Grey roads have no reading in the last 10 minutes. Where only buses report, the speed is a bus speed between stops, which runs below car speed.

      How it works
      • Road colours are absolute speeds, not a ratio to a baseline (step 6 of the plan).
      • Where a Caltrans detector sits on a segment, its speed is shown (cars, deprecated ATMS feed). Elsewhere the colour is the speed of buses between stops: pairs of vehicle positions 30 s apart projected onto the route shape, with pairs near a stop, under 20 m, over 120 s apart or over 110 km/h dropped.
      • A segment's value is the length-weighted mean of the last 10 minutes. Hover or tap a road to see where it came from.
      • Vehicles: positions as the agencies publish them, up to 5 minutes old, LA Metro over its WebSocket, others polled every 30 s. On the GitHub Pages copy the snapshots come from a workflow about every 5 minutes; the status pill says "Scheduled" and positions are as old as the snapshot.
      • Events: CHP incidents of the Los Angeles dispatch, Caltrans lane closures current now, in-service message signs. Camera stills come from Caltrans when a popup opens; nothing is proxied.
      • Camera counts: a separate sampler opens one Caltrans stream at a time, decodes 15 s, tracks vehicles with YOLO11 small and ByteTrack, and counts tracks crossing a fixed line in each direction. Directions A and B are the two sides of that line, not route directions. Speed needs a per-camera ground calibration; none exists yet. Some streams loop old footage; the burned-in clock on the frame is the only sign (docs/log/step7.md).
      • Speeds are shown in mph with km/h after them; the data is kept in km/h. Times are Pacific.
      • Terrain is the AWS Terrain Tiles DEM with 1.3 times exaggeration. Buildings are OSM footprints extruded by height, else levels times 3.5 m, else 8 m.
